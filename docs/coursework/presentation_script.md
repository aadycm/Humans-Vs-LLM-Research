# Presentation script: ENVSE 497 Data Collection Review (Nov 2026)

1. **Title.** I'm Aadithya; my project is about how framing affects risky decisions, in people and in AI models. Today: what data I got, what it looks like, what went well or badly collecting it.
2. **The study.** Four scenarios: gain or loss framing, with clear or vague odds. People open up to 4 of 7 info cards, then pick a safe or a risky option. Same scenarios given to students and three GPT models.
3. **What I collected.** 212 responses; 14 who rushed through removed, leaving 198; plus 360 AI runs. I was aiming for 50.
4. **Recruitment.** 204 of 212 took it on October 27, the day extra credit was announced in class. Great for numbers, but most people came from one place.
5. **Who took part.** Mostly first- and second-year students, mostly CS, IST and engineering; two-thirds no work experience. Fine for a first study, not practicing engineers.
6. **Time and quality.** About 7 minutes against the 15 I estimated; a few left it open for hours, so I use time per decision. After fixing bugs before launch, nothing is missing.
7. **Engagement dropped.** By the last scenario half opened no cards; decisions went from about a minute to about 15 seconds. Fixed order means tiredness can't be fully separated from the conditions.
8. **The AI data.** Two planned GPT models were retired, so I swapped in newer ones. AIs almost always opened all 4 cards, mostly the same ones. GPT-4.1 and GPT-4o chose safe every time. GPT-4o-mini chose safe in all 60 gain-worded runs but only 24 of 60 loss-worded runs (40%); people moved the same way but less, 67% to 56%.
9. **What worked.** Far more people than planned; testing caught bugs early; cleaning rules set in advance; shared survey with Danah; anonymous; automated AI runs.
10. **What didn't.** Qualtrics dropped data until fixed; fixed order; one recruitment channel; wrong time estimate; retired models.
11. **Next time.** Shuffle order, recruit from more places, make AIs pick fewer cards, keep testing before launch. Data is done and clean; full results in the final report.

**Limitations, if asked:** fixed order (fatigue tangled with conditions); narrow sample; one scenario per condition; model swap and AIs always opening 4 cards.
**Results, if asked:** framing changed what people chose, not what information they looked at.
